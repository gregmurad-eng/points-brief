Lead Story

The Big Three Finish Converging — and United Makes the Card the Point

The 2026 loyalty landscape has quietly settled into a single shape. Delta SkyMiles and American AAdvantage now sit on an identical 5-to-11-miles-per-dollar earning ladder, and United MileagePlus has taken the same revenue-based logic one step further. Since United's April overhaul, the program effectively splits members into two tiers: those who carry a United co-branded card and those who don't.

The penalty is real. Base members without a United card now earn just three miles per eligible dollar on airfare — roughly 60% of what Delta and American hand out at their entry rungs. The message from all three carriers is the same one they've been inching toward for a decade: your spending pattern, not your flight map, determines your balance, and the co-branded card is no longer a nice-to-have but the load-bearing wall of the whole structure.

For Greg's stack, the practical read is that United miles are increasingly a card-earned currency, while Delta and American reward the same paid-fare behavior at parity. If you're choosing where a marginal paid flight goes, the differentiator in 2026 is which co-brand you hold, not which alliance flies the route.
