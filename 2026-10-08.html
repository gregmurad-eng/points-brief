Today's Transfer Bonuses

Chase Ultimate Rewards → Marriott Bonvoy — 70% (expires Oct 15)

Window: Sept 15 – Oct 15, 2026 · Ratio: 1,000 UR = 1,700 Bonvoy · This is the highest Chase → Marriott bonus ever recorded.[1][2][3]

Marriott's Reasonable Redemption Value sits near 0.7 cent per point after the 2026 program-wide devaluation. A 70% bonus pushes effective value only when you've already identified a specific redemption that beats the paid rate: Category 1–4 off-peak, Ritz-Carlton Reserve on a long stay where the fifth night free compounds the bonus, or a Hoshinoya/St. Regis property pricing out in the thousands of dollars per night. Transferring speculatively into Marriott at any bonus is a well-documented way to end up with 400,000 Bonvoy points you cannot spend well.[4]

Your move: Transfer only if you have a specific Marriott award priced and ideally held. Do not stockpile Bonvoy at 1:1.7; the next Chase → Marriott bonus of comparable size is unlikely before Q2 2027 but Marriott inventory and redemption rates are the risk, not the ratio.

Amex Membership Rewards → Hilton Honors — 30% (expires Oct 14)

Window: Sept 1 – Oct 14, 2026 · Ratio: 1,000 MR = 2,600 Hilton.[1][5][6]

Hilton Honors points have dropped to roughly 0.35 cent in trade-side valuations, so the 30% bonus turns an MR point into about 0.91 cent of Hilton value — worse than cashing out MR for travel at 1 cent via Pay with Points on any premium Amex. The bonus looks big and prices small. The exceptions are Conrad Maldives, Waldorf Astoria Maldives, and a handful of Caribbean peak-season Waldorfs where paid rates break $2,000 per night and even depressed Hilton points still clear 1 cent.[4]

Your move: Skip unless you are booking a specific Waldorf or Conrad luxury property where paid rates make the math work. "30%" on a devalued currency is still a devalued currency.

Amex Membership Rewards → Air France-KLM Flying Blue — 25% (expires Oct 31)

Window: Oct 1 – Oct 31, 2026 · Ratio: 1,000 MR = 1,250 Flying Blue.[1][7][8]

Flying Blue's September 8 tiered-fare rollout bumped many award prices; a 25% bonus brings the effective MR cost roughly back to pre-change levels on the lower fare tiers. The best use remains Flying Blue Promo Rewards, with October 2026 month-ahead promos running as low as 18,750 miles each way in economy between Europe and 14 North American cities, bookable through March 2027.[9]

Your move: Transfer only after confirming the specific Flying Blue award is bookable at the price you expect. Transferred Flying Blue miles expire if you don't earn or redeem for 24 months and no Flying Blue mile has ever moved back to Amex.

Amex Membership Rewards → Aeromexico Rewards — 20% (expires Oct 30)

Ratio: 1,000 MR = 1,920 Aeromexico (base ratio is 1:1.6).[1][4]

Aeromexico is a SkyTeam partner with occasional sweet spots on Delta One between the US and Mexico when SkyMiles pricing goes stratospheric. The 1:1.6 base ratio is already richer than most Amex partners; the 20% bonus is a reason to look if you already have an Aeromexico redemption in mind, not a reason to go hunting.

Your move: Only transfer if you have a Delta or Aeromexico-operated SkyTeam award held or price-locked; the bonus is a tiebreaker, not a thesis.

Citi ThankYou → Japan Airlines Mileage Bank — 30% (expires Oct 24)

Launched Sept 20, 2026 alongside JAL joining Citi as a 1:1 partner. Ratio with bonus: annual-fee cards 1,000 TY = 1,300 JAL; no-annual-fee cards 1,000 TY = 910 JAL.[10][11][12]

The real sweet spot is JAL first class to Tokyo at 70,000–80,000 miles each way one-way from the US — after the 30% bonus, that's ~54,000 Citi TY points for a $17,000 paid fare. JAL has limited award space but releases some ~330 days out and sporadically closer in. The no-annual-fee ratio makes the bonus a net loss; you need Strata Elite, Strata Premier, or Prestige.

Your move: If you hold a premium Citi card AND have JAL award space in hand, transfer. Otherwise wait — this is a launch promo and will likely cycle again, but JAL space is the actual gating constraint.

Citi ThankYou → Avianca LifeMiles — 25% (expires Oct 24)

Window: Sept 20 – Oct 24, 2026 · Premium cards: 1,000 TY = 1,250 LifeMiles; others 1,000 TY = 875.[1][13][14]

LifeMiles remains the cheapest way to book Star Alliance business class for cash-plus-miles users, with no fuel surcharges. The historical catch: LifeMiles expires after 12 months of account inactivity and the program has a long history of IT outages mid-booking. Transferred miles are irrevocable.

Your move: Transfer only with a specific Star Alliance redemption already price-checked on the LifeMiles calendar; and only if you have a premium Citi card (Strata Elite, Strata Premier, Prestige). The 1:0.875 ratio on no-annual-fee cards still loses value even with the bonus.

Citi ThankYou → Qatar Airways Privilege Club — up to 35% (expires Oct 31)

Tiered by transfer size, offered by Qatar not Citi: 15% on <50k Avios, 25% on 50k–99,999, 35% on 100k+ in a single transfer. Posts by Nov 30, 2026.[15][16][17]

Qatar Avios are the fastest route to Qsuite redemptions — the Oneworld business class people actually scheme for. The tier structure punishes small transfers; a 49,999 Avios transfer gets 15% and a 100,000 Avios transfer gets 35%. Note this bonus is run by Qatar Airways, not Citi, which is why it's not on the Citi transfer-partner page itself. Transferred Avios also move 1:1 between British Airways, Iberia, Aer Lingus, Finnair, and Qatar within the Avios ecosystem.

Your move: Only maximize the 35% tier if you need 100k+ Avios for a specific Qsuite or long-haul Oneworld redemption. For smaller amounts the Capital One → British Airways 20% (below) often beats this, since BA Avios transfer to Qatar 1:1.

Capital One Miles → British Airways Club — 20% (expires Oct 31)

Window: Oct 1 – Oct 31, 2026 · Ratio: 1,000 miles = 1,200 Avios.[1][18][19]

BA Avios flex within the Avios ecosystem — land them at BA and move to Iberia, Aer Lingus, Qatar, or Finnair 1:1. For an American-centric Avios play, Aer Lingus to Dublin (business class from the East Coast ~62,500 Avios each way) and short-haul Oneworld domestic hops remain the enduring value. BA's own direct US routes are expensive after surcharges unless you're flying Reward Flight Saver on a shorter segment.

Your move: If you were already going to move Capital One miles into the Avios system, do it this month — the 20% effectively discounts the Avios ecosystem across five programs. If you weren't, don't let the bonus pull the trigger.

Rove Miles — Flying Blue 40%, JAL 50%, Qatar up to 35% (all expire Oct 31)

Rove is a smaller fintech-adjacent transferable program. Three stacked October bonuses.[2][1]

Rove Miles are harder to earn in volume than Amex, Chase, Citi, or Capital One points — most readers will have small balances and the ratios are the exception rather than the rule for cardholders. If you have Rove balance idle and a Flying Blue, JAL, or Qatar redemption, the ratios are market-leading for October.

Your move: Only relevant if you hold a Rove balance already. Rove → JAL at 1:1.5 is the standout if the JAL space is there.

Marriott Bonvoy valuation drift

TPG's October 2026 valuations put Bonvoy at 0.75 cent and NerdWallet at 0.70 cent after this year's program-wide devaluation.[23][22] This is the context behind the Chase 70% bonus: Marriott needs the inflow and the record-high conversion ratio only partly offsets the devalued output.

Your move: Treat Bonvoy as a redeem-fast currency, not a hoard-and-wait one. Property-level flash devaluations remain the biggest unhedged risk in the program.
