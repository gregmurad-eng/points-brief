Today's Transfer Bonuses

Chase Ultimate Rewards → IHG One Rewards

80% · ends Dec 3, 2026 · ratio 1:1.8 (base 1:1)

The headline bonus of the month. 1,000 Ultimate Rewards points become 1,800 IHG One Rewards points. The math looks huge, but IHG points typically redeem at roughly 0.5¢ each, so 1,800 IHG is worth about 0.9¢ per original Chase point — less than keeping them as UR (~1.5¢+ as cash-equivalent) unless you have a specific high-value IHG stay in mind, especially a PointBreaks property or a long Vignette Collection award.[4][6]

Your move: Only transfer if you have an IHG award already identified and the paid rate is above $280/night. Otherwise, leave as Chase points — the 80% is a volume bonus for a weak partner, not a free lunch.

Amex Membership Rewards → Marriott Bonvoy

35% · ends Dec 10, 2026 · ratio 1:1.35 (base 1:1)

A quieter promo that is probably the most useful hotel transfer running right now for Greg's stack. 1,000 Amex MR becomes 1,350 Bonvoy. Marriott's late-2026 award-chart creep (5–10% quietly hiked[10]) is a real argument for holding Amex points — but if you've got a specific redemption in a Category 6+ property, the arithmetic finally tips to transferring.[2]

Your move: Transfer only for an in-hand Marriott redemption at a property where the paid rate exceeds ~0.9¢/Bonvoy-point — roughly $450+/night on a 50K award. Never speculate into Bonvoy; quiet devaluations punish that.

American Express Membership Rewards → Virgin Atlantic Flying Club

30% · ends Nov 30, 2026 · ratio 1:1.3 (base 1:1)

Virgin Atlantic is the sleeper partner of the Amex transfer line — it's a SkyTeam-adjacent program that still prices ANA first-class round-trip at 110K miles one-way and Delta One to Europe at reasonable cash co-pays off-peak. With the 30%, 1,000 MR becomes 1,300 Flying Club.[1]

Your move: Transfer only against a held award space on Delta, ANA, or Air France that has already been confirmed as bookable. Flying Club mileage is useless speculatively — cash co-pays and phone-booking friction eat the margin.

Chase Ultimate Rewards → British Airways / Iberia / Aer Lingus Avios

30% · ends Dec 1, 2026 · ratio 1:1.3 (base 1:1)

A rare 30% across all three IAG Avios programs simultaneously — and Avios remain one of the strongest short-haul currencies for domestic AA flights (4,500 Avios, LAX–SFO one-way) and transatlantic in premium economy on Iberia. 1,000 UR = 1,300 Avios.[5][6]

Your move: Top up to a known award: an AA short-haul, an Iberia off-peak transatlantic, or an Aer Lingus Dublin routing. If you don't have a specific flight in mind, skip — Avios are a tool, not a store of value.

Amex Membership Rewards → Hilton Honors

40% · ends Nov 24, 2026 · ratio 1:2.8 (base 1:2)

A genuinely rare promo — Amex hadn't run a Hilton bonus of this size since 2022.[3] 1,000 MR becomes 2,800 Hilton Honors points. Hilton's point value is thin (roughly 0.4–0.5¢ each), so on a per-MR-point basis this lands around 1.1–1.4¢ — competitive only if you have an in-hand Hilton stay at an aspirational property or you need to top up an exact award balance.

Your move: Transfer only to complete a specific 5th-night-free Hilton award or a top-tier property at Waldorf/Conrad. Don't convert to Hilton as a hedge — Hilton points are less flexible than MR and arrive in a weakening currency.

Citi ThankYou → Cathay Pacific Asia Miles

10% · ends Nov 14, 2026 · ratio 1:1.1 (base 1:1) · 3 days left

A small bonus but the only live Cathay Pacific transfer bonus. Asia Miles remain one of the best ways to book oneworld business class — JFK–HKG at 85,000 one-way, HKG–SYD at 68,000. 1,000 Citi = 1,100 Asia Miles.[7]

Your move: Only transfer if Cathay or AA award space is already on hold in your Asia Miles account — the 10% is too small to justify speculative conversion, and Asia Miles expire 18 months from last activity.

Program Watch

World of Hyatt: Chase UR → Hyatt now 4:3 for Preferred/Ink cards

Since October 1, 2026, Chase Sapphire Preferred and Ink Business Preferred holders transfer to World of Hyatt at 4:3 — a 25% cut from the long-standing 1:1. Only Chase Sapphire Reserve and Sapphire Reserve for Business still transfer at 1:1. This is the single largest structural change in the US transferable-points landscape this year and makes "which card earned this UR point" a meaningful question for the first time in a decade.[9]

Your move: Audit which of your UR balances came from a Reserve vs a Preferred/Ink. If you hold both, route Hyatt transfers from the Reserve-earned pool and reserve the Preferred-earned pool for 1:1 partners (United, Southwest, Marriott). This is a one-time cleanup that's worth doing now.

Marriott Bonvoy: unannounced 5–10% award-chart creep

Third-party trackers detected award costs across the Marriott portfolio rising 5–10% in late 2026 with no formal announcement from Marriott and no email to members.[10] Average Bonvoy point value has drifted from ~0.84¢ in 2024 to roughly 0.7–0.8¢ now. Owners-group pressure on the program's economics suggests more downward pressure in 2027.

Your move: If you were holding Bonvoy for a future stay, price-check the award now — the quiet revaluations make "I'll book later" the worst strategy in the program. Known stay? Book it. Speculative hoard? Reconsider.

United MileagePlus: 2026 saver-award increases

United's own April 2026 release described an 18% average saver increase; AwardWallet's independent tracking puts the real figure at closer to 33% on United metal, with partner business-class saver availability on United-operated flights falling 22% year-over-year.[11] Pricing is dynamic and no award chart is published — this is a process story, not a single event.

Your move: For any Star Alliance redemption, price it in United miles, Aeroplan, and LifeMiles before transferring. On transpacific business, the partner charts increasingly beat United direct — don't default to MileagePlus just because Chase transfers 1:1.

Today's Play

The 80% headline isn't the best deal in the market. Chase → IHG at 1:1.8 is a volume bonus for a weak currency. Amex → Marriott at 35% buys a stronger hotel point, and Chase → IAG Avios at 30% buys the most flexible airline point of the month (good on AA short-haul, Iberia transatlantic, and Aer Lingus Dublin routings).

If you must transfer today: pick the bonus that funds a specific booking you've already researched. The one-way, irreversible nature of these transfers means that doing nothing is a legitimate choice — and in a landscape where Hyatt just cut its Chase ratio and Marriott is quietly inflating awards, being liquid in UR and MR is itself a position.
