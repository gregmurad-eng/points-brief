Today's Transfer Bonuses

Amex → British Airways / Iberia / Aer Lingus Avios — 30%

Expires: Sunday, September 27, 2026 (2 days) · Ratio: 1,000 MR → 1,300 Avios[1][2]

Amex has been running this three-way Avios bonus since August 3. The Avios family sits at the centre of the Amex stack because Iberia short-haul to Madrid and single-partner American Airlines short-haul remain two of the strongest sweet spots reachable directly from Membership Rewards. Historical cadence says the next 25%+ Amex → Avios bonus lands sometime in Q4 or early Q1 — a repeat is likely, though timing is not guaranteed.

Your move: Transfer only if you have a specific Avios redemption already priced (Iberia short-haul, AA domestic in first, Aer Lingus transatlantic) and confirmed available for your dates. Do not speculatively top up an Avios pool — the family devalues in flashes and unbooked Avios are the ones that get cut.

Chase → Marriott Bonvoy — 70% (record-high)

Expires: Thursday, October 15, 2026 · Ratio: 1,000 UR → 1,700 Bonvoy[3][4]

Chase's largest-ever transfer bonus to Marriott Bonvoy, live since September 15 and matching the previous all-time high. Multiple trade desks describe this as an outlier headline event rather than the start of an elevated cadence — the same read our ledger is tracking (call 2026-09-16-1). The bonus is genuinely large; the underlying redemption economics are not: even at 1,000 UR → 1,700 Bonvoy, the effective per-point value of Ultimate Rewards spent on Marriott hovers around 1.2 cents, well below what those same UR fetch through Hyatt at 1:1 or through Air Canada Aeroplan for a well-timed award.

Your move: Only transfer if you have a specific Marriott stay booked (or a specific award-chart property targeted with confirmed availability) that mathematically comes out ahead of the UR alternatives you'd otherwise use for the same trip. This is the wrong move for anyone with Chase Sapphire Preferred or Ink Business Preferred who still needs to lock in a Hyatt 1:1 transfer before October 1 (see Program Watch below) — that clock is louder.

Chase → Air Canada Aeroplan — 20% (30% for Aeroplan cardholders)

Expires: Tuesday, September 30, 2026 (5 days) · Ratio: 1,000 UR → 1,200 Aeroplan (1,300 for Aeroplan Chase cardholders)[2][5]

The Aeroplan promo has been Chase's only UR → airline transfer bonus of 2026 so far and is our ledger's basis for expecting Chase to stay quiet on airline promos through Q4 (call 2026-08-24-1). Aeroplan remains one of the strongest Star Alliance award programs reachable via a US bank — its distance-based partner chart still prices US-to-Europe business under 70,000 miles on many routes.

Your move: Transfer only against a specific Aeroplan award with confirmed availability. Aeroplan sales taxes and carrier surcharges on partner awards vary widely by carrier — always price the total (miles + cash) before pulling the trigger. If you hold no immediate Aeroplan target, let this expire; a fresh Chase → Aeroplan bonus is unlikely again in 2026, but Aeroplan's own website consistently runs its own partner-flavour promotions.

Amex → Hilton Honors — 30%

Expires: Wednesday, October 14, 2026 · Ratio: 1,000 MR → 2,600 Hilton[1][2]

Hilton is Amex's least valuable per-point hotel transfer destination on a straight cents-per-point basis (~0.5c per Hilton point vs ~0.7c per Bonvoy point, and multiples more if the same MR were transferred to Aeroplan or Virgin Atlantic for an aspirational award). The 30% bonus is genuinely large — but even at the boosted ratio, MR spent on Hilton points is Amex's weakest-value transfer choice most of the time.

Your move: Use this to top up a specific Hilton stay where a five-figure Hilton point balance is already close to the redemption target and you can't get an award-space alternative on Hyatt or a Bonvoy Ritz-Carlton. Do not speculatively transfer to build a Hilton pool — Hilton's dynamic pricing has drifted upward monotonically through 2026.

Only transfer against a specific JAL redemption target (its own First Class awards are notably good value). JAL Mileage Bank miles expire 36 months from issue with no reset — do not accumulate speculatively.

Program Watch

Chase → Hyatt 4:3 devaluation lands for Sapphire Preferred & Ink Business Preferred — Oct 1 (6 days)

On October 1, existing Chase Sapphire Preferred and Chase Ink Business Preferred cardholders lose their 1:1 transfer rate to World of Hyatt and drop to 4:3 — a 25% effective devaluation on the single most valuable Chase transfer partner. Sapphire Reserve and Sapphire Reserve for Business retain 1:1.[10] Chase announced this on June 15 as part of the Sapphire Preferred refresh; the ~110-day notice window is unusually long. Our ledger (call 2026-09-18-1) predicts Chase does not walk this back.

Your move: If you hold Sapphire Preferred or Ink Business Preferred AND you have a specific Hyatt stay you can book or hold before October 1, transferring the exact number of points needed for that redemption at 1:1 is defensible. Do not speculatively dump your entire UR balance into Hyatt — Chase and Hyatt continue to fight over what happens to unbooked balances, and speculative dumps under a deadline are historically the worst redemption decision most points-holders make in a given year.

Bilt has announced its October 1 Rent Day promotion: Hilton Honors transfer bonuses ranging from 75% (Blue) to 200% (Platinum + $200 Bilt Cash buy-up), plus instant Hilton elite status through year-end 2026 for anyone who transfers; separately, Amtrak Guest Rewards transfer bonuses of 25% (Blue) to 125% (Platinum + $400 Bilt Cash buy-up).[11] Standard ratios are 1:1 for Hilton and 2:1 for Amtrak. Flying Blue Light / Standard / Flex — direct AF & KLM awards live since Sept 8

Flying Blue split direct Air France and KLM awards into three tiers on September 8, 2026. Business-class Paris–New York now prices at 60,000 miles (Light), 75,000 miles (Standard), or 110,000 miles (Flex) one-way, versus a previous single ~60,000-mile entry point. Light drops lounge access and one checked bag and is non-refundable; Standard adds those back for a modest change fee. Partner-airline pricing is officially unchanged.[12] Our ledger tracks whether partner pricing stays protected (call 2026-09-02-1) and whether a bank will run a smoothing 20%+ Flying Blue bonus inside 30 days of the change (call 2026-09-08-1) — 17 days in with no such bonus yet.

Your move: If you already hold Flying Blue miles for direct AF/KLM business, price your target both ways — Light plus paid seat/luggage often ends up worse than Standard when totalled. If you were holding a large Flying Blue balance speculatively, this is the correction moment — either book something soon or hold and watch for a bank transfer bonus to smooth the pain.

Today's Play

Two clocks are running past each other this week — do not let the louder one drown out the more expensive one.

The 70% Chase → Marriott bonus is the loud clock: it's a record, it's on every trade site, and it has three weeks left. The 4:3 Chase → Hyatt devaluation for Sapphire Preferred and Ink Business Preferred cardholders is the more expensive clock: it lands in six days and, for anyone who holds those cards and had counted on 1:1 Hyatt for a specific booking, the switching cost is real.

Rank your decisions by irreversibility, not by percentage. Any 1:1 Hyatt transfer against a specific, held Hyatt booking made before October 1 preserves a rate you cannot get back. Any 70% Marriott transfer made without a specific booking in mind is speculation — and Marriott has spent 2026 quietly moving properties into higher point-cost bands (see today's ledger call).

The Amex → Avios expires in two days. If you have a specific Iberia or AA short-haul award on hold, this is a genuinely time-sensitive transfer. If you do not, let it expire — Amex will run another Avios bonus by end of year (that is our ledger call 2026-08-19-1).
