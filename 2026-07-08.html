Lead Story

Virgin Atlantic is the summer’s quiet workhorse — and Amex is paying you 30% to notice

With the headline Chase-to-Marriott 55% bonus gone at the end of June, the most useful offer left standing for Greg’s stack is a 30% transfer bonus from American Express Membership Rewards to Virgin Atlantic Flying Club, running through July 31. Chase Ultimate Rewards has carried a matching 30% Virgin Atlantic bonus this month as well, though its window closes earlier in July, so the Amex side is the one with room to breathe.

Virgin’s appeal was never Virgin’s own metal — it’s the partner chart. Flying Club miles book Delta One, ANA first and business to Tokyo, and Air France-KLM across the Atlantic, often for far fewer miles than the operating carrier charges directly. A 30% bump turns 100,000 flexible points into 130,000 Flying Club miles, which is frequently the difference between one premium seat and two. The usual discipline applies: transfers are one-way and instant, so move points only once an award seat is confirmed on hold.

Since April 2, MileagePlus base members without a United co-branded card earn just 3 miles per dollar — roughly 60% of what Delta and American now hand out. If your United spending runs through a Chase-issued United card, you’re insulated; if not, this is the nudge to reconsider where paid United fares get charged. Details.

The Signal

If you have a premium transatlantic or trans-Pacific seat in mind for fall, price it through Virgin Atlantic Flying Club before July 31 and route the miles from Amex at the 30% bonus — but confirm the award seat first, then transfer. Don’t speculatively move points for a bonus; move them for a booking.
