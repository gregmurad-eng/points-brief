Today’s Transfer Bonuses

Six active bonuses today. Every percentage below is corroborated across at least two independent trade sources; the underlying program pages are cited in Sources. Standard disclaimer — a transfer is one-way and irreversible. Move points only for a specific redemption you have priced and (ideally) held.

Amex Membership Rewards → Hilton Honors — 30%[1][2]

Expires October 14, 2026 · Ratio 1,000 MR = 2,600 Hilton points (standard 1:2)

Big headline number, weak underlying currency. Hilton points are Amex’s lowest-value hotel transfer destination on a per-point basis, and the boosted ratio doesn’t change that math for most redemptions.

Your move: transfer only if you already have a specific top-tier Waldorf-Astoria or Conrad stay priced out and confirmed available — not to bank Hilton points for “someday.”

Amex Membership Rewards → British Airways, Iberia, and Aer Lingus Avios — 30%[1][2]

Expires September 27, 2026 · Ratio 1,000 MR = 1,300 Avios (standard 1:1)

The reliable Avios pump. Iberia short-haul from Madrid (7,500 Avios in economy on the shortest routes) and American Airlines partner short-haul via BA are the two redemptions this bonus is designed to unlock. Aer Lingus off-peak transatlantic economy is a distant third useful case.

Your move: transfer only if you have confirmed award space on a specific Iberia or AA short-haul ticket — Avios balances outside a booking sit and lose value to eventual devaluation.

Chase Ultimate Rewards → Air Canada Aeroplan — 20% (30% for co-brand cardholders)[1][2]

Expires September 30, 2026 · Ratio 1,000 UR = 1,200 Aeroplan (1,300 with co-brand)

Chase’s one 2026 airline bonus, and it’s a good one. Aeroplan’s mixed-cabin distance-based partner chart survives the recent Blackstone-investment noise (see Program Watch), and Star Alliance business class North America to Europe from 60,000 miles remains reachable. The 30% requires an Aeroplan-branded card in your wallet; the 20% is open to any UR holder.

Your move: transfer for a specific Star Alliance business-class hold you have already priced in Aeroplan — don’t speculate on future award space.

Citi ThankYou → Leaders Club (Leading Hotels of the World) — 25%[3][2]

Expires September 19, 2026 · Ratio 1,000 TY = 250 LHW points (Strata Elite / Strata Premier / Prestige only; 1,000 TY = 175 on other Citi cards)

Niche. Leaders Club is the loyalty program for the independent-luxury Leading Hotels of the World collective. Redemption math only works at specific properties, and even then the value ceiling is modest. Not a routine transfer target.

Your move: no action for the vast majority of readers. Only relevant if you already had a specific LHW property in mind and are holding the right Citi card — otherwise skip.

Rove Miles → Copa Airlines ConnectMiles — 40%[4][5]

Expires September 30, 2026 · Ratio 1,000 Rove = 1,400 ConnectMiles · Minimum 2,000-mile transfer

Rove is a newer transferable-points program and this is the launch bonus for its Copa partnership. ConnectMiles is a lesser-known Star Alliance program whose principal virtue is not passing fuel surcharges on many partner awards. Award availability must be confirmed directly with Copa — do not rely on another carrier’s calendar to gauge Copa space.

Your move: only if you already hold Rove miles and have a specific Star Alliance itinerary priced in ConnectMiles with space confirmed by phone. Not a reason to start earning Rove today.

Capital One Miles → JAL Mileage Bank — 30%[1][2]

Expires September 30, 2026 · Trade sources agree on 30% bonus; ratio should confirm as 1,000 Cap One = 1,300 JAL, but verify in the Capital One rewards portal before executing

JAL Mileage Bank is a distance-based Oneworld program with excellent business-class sweet spots on JAL itself (and pain-in-the-neck rules on partners). This is the first Capital One→JAL bonus in some time. Award space on JAL long-haul business is famously scarce and needs to be locked before transfer.

One source (The Points Guy’s ratio table) lists the post-bonus ratio inconsistently. The 30% headline is well-corroborated; the ratio math is not. Do not transfer speculatively — open the Capital One portal, initiate the transfer, and read the exact ratio shown before you confirm.

Your move: only if you have a confirmed JAL business-class hold and the post-bonus ratio in-app matches what you expect. Otherwise pass.

Program Watch

Flying Blue: tiered award fares go live September 8

Effective September 8, 2026 · Direct Air France / KLM awards only; partner airlines unchanged[6][7]

Flying Blue splits every direct AF/KLM award into three fare tiers: Light (current entry-level pricing but stripped of flexibility, seat assignments, and lounge access on premium cabins), Standard (roughly matches today’s inclusions, at roughly 25–30% more miles), and Flex (fully flexible, cheaper cash fees). Published examples: Paris–New York business class at Light 60,000 / Standard 75,000 / Flex 85,000. Flying Blue Platinum members and above are largely spared. Partner-airline award pricing is explicitly unchanged — a critical carve-out that is the subject of today’s ledger call below.

Marriott Q4 2026 promotion: leaked, not yet launched

Rumor. That’s a modest offer by Bonvoy’s own recent history — well below the “double points” promos of prior years. Wait for official launch before making a booking decision on the strength of this.

Q1 2026 (Feb 2 – Apr 15) and a Summer promo (Jul 1 – Sep 7) both ran; Q4 silence is notable[9]

Hyatt’s summer “up to 8,000 bonus points” promo runs out this week. In prior years a Q4 Bonus Journeys has landed by mid-September; nothing announced as of today. Today’s Play

Six bonuses, one calendar squeeze — and one reason not to move

September is bonus-heavy: Amex is running two, Chase and Rove and Capital One and Citi each running one. But note the expiries — five of the six are gone by October 1. That is the signal to book-then-transfer, not transfer-then-hope. If you already have a specific award ticket priced out and space confirmed, this is the week. If you don’t, sit still: unmoved points in a bank program stay flexible; miles in an airline account get eaten by the next devaluation.

Case in point: Flying Blue’s September 8 tiered rollout is a live demonstration of why speculative transfers punish you. Anyone who moved MR to Flying Blue in July on the strength of “great sweet spots” is now looking at Light fares with no changes, no seat assignment, no lounge access — unless they pay another 25–30% in miles to reach Standard. The points you didn’t transfer are the points you still control.
