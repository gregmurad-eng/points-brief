Chase Ultimate Rewards → IHG One Rewards — 70%

Ratio: 1,000 UR → 1,700 IHG points · Expires: August 31, 2026[3][4]

Optically large, mathematically weak. Chase Ultimate Rewards points are worth roughly 1.75 cents each on a considered redemption; IHG points are worth roughly 0.4–0.5 cents each. Even with the 70% bonus, you're trading down from ~1.75 cents to ~0.8 cents per point. The only case this pencils is a specific, high-cash-price IHG award night at an Intercontinental you were already planning to book — and even then, the cash-vs-points math usually favours cash right now.

Your move: no action for almost everyone. Consider only if you have a confirmed IHG award booking priced at 1.5 cents or better per Chase point after the bonus — a rare combination. Otherwise hold the Chase points; they compound better elsewhere.

Hyatt — post-May 2026 five-band chart is settling in, and the top end has moved sharply

Effective: May 20, 2026[12][13]

Hyatt's move from a three-band (off-peak / standard / peak) chart to a five-band (Lowest / Low / Moderate / Upper / Top) chart landed in May and its consequences are now visible in the wild: the old maximum of 45,000 points/night has become 75,000 at the top-band aspirational properties, and some Category 8 hotels have seen effective per-night pricing rise up to 67%. The chart is still published (rare among major hotel programs), which is the win to hold onto. But the ceiling is higher and the "peak" band is no longer the ceiling.

Your move: if you hold Chase UR for Hyatt aspirational stays, price your target dates now in the Hyatt engine — the chart is public and the number you see is what you'll pay. Do not stockpile UR for Hyatt on an assumed old-ceiling pricing.

Marriott Bonvoy — quiet mid-year award rate increases across the portfolio

Rolling: early July 2026 onward[14][15]

Marriott raised award pricing across many properties in early July with no advance notice, with individual hotels going up 2.4% to 15.6% (most in the 3–8% range). The St. Regis Maldives Vommuli introduced a new Category-8-plus band pricing at up to 120,000 Bonvoy points/night — the first time Marriott has published a redemption rate above the prior 100K peak ceiling. This is dynamic-pricing behaviour in a program that technically retains a chart.

Your move: no immediate transfer action — Marriott doesn't accept most transfer-partner points at competitive ratios anyway. Book Marriott awards you're planning against sooner rather than later; the trend is upward.

American Airlines AAdvantage — partner earning bonus capped at 25% from 60K Loyalty Points

2026 program year[16]

The prior structure — a 20% Loyalty Point bonus after 60K LP and 30% after 100K on eligible partner transactions — has been consolidated to a single 25% bonus after 60K LP. This is a quiet devaluation for high-earners who used to reach 30%. The partner award chart itself remains untouched, which matters more: Qatar Qsuite, JAL, Cathay, and BA still price at published rates. AA has now held the partner chart flat for a decade, but the earning-side changes suggest the redemption chart may be next in line.

Your move: if you have high-value AA partner awards in mind (Qsuite, JAL First), price them and book while the chart stands. If you're grinding Loyalty Points through partners, model your 2026 earn on the flat 25% rate above 60K rather than the old stepped structure.

Today's Play

The move today is: probably don't move points. Four of the six live bonuses are either too small (LifeMiles 15%), mathematically weak (IHG 70%), too routine to chase without a booking (Avios 30%, Aeroplan 20%), or expiring in 72 hours with dynamic-pricing risk (Flying Blue 20%). The one bonus where the math is genuinely strong — Citi → Turkish at 40% — sits on a program with real booking friction and stranded-points risk.

A points brief that tells you to transfer every time a bonus is live is selling you the illusion of action. The correct action most days is none: keep your points liquid, and move them the day you can hold or confirm a specific award. Transfer bonuses are the airline's marketing spend to move balances off your issuer's books and onto theirs. They benefit from movement; you benefit only from the specific award you book.

Exceptions today: (1) if you already have a Turkish agent hold on a partner business-class award, the 40% bonus makes it worth breaking the hold-and-transfer sequence today. (2) If you're a Chase Aeroplan cardholder with a specific Aeroplan booking and room under the 25K bonus cap, the effective 30% is a real number. (3) If the Amex Avios bonus lines up with a booked or held Iberia or AA short-haul award, transfer the exact number and no more.
