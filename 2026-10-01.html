Today's Transfer Bonuses

Bilt Rewards → Hilton Honors — up to 200%[1]

Expires: October 1, 2026 (today only, 23:59 PT) · Base ratio: 1:1 · Bonus caps at 100,000 bonus points per member

Bilt's October Rent Day puts an elevated transfer bonus on Hilton that scales with Bilt elite tier: 75% for Blue, 100% for Silver, 150% for Gold, 175% for Platinum, and 200% if you upgrade to Platinum Plus with Bilt Cash. At 200% the effective rate is 1:3 — one Bilt Point becomes three Hilton Honors. Hilton is a thin per-point redemption (roughly 0.5c), so even at 1:3 you are moving points worth about 1.5c each.[2]

Your move: Transfer only if you already hold a confirmed aspirational Hilton redemption — Conrad Maldives, Waldorf Maldives, Conrad Bora Bora — where the per-night point cost crushes cash. For a run-of-the-mill US Hampton stay the maths does not work even at 200%; take Bilt's one-cent floor via Amtrak instead.

Bilt Rewards → Amtrak Guest Rewards — up to 125%[1]

Expires: October 1, 2026 (today only, 23:59 PT) · Base ratio: 1:1

Second Rent Day lever: Amtrak at up to 125% depending on Bilt tier. Amtrak Guest Rewards price Acela and Northeast Regional awards against cash fare at roughly 2.5–3c per point on peak-cash routes, which is why this bonus matters even though Amtrak is less glamorous than the Hilton number.[3]

Your move: Only transfer if you have an Acela or peak-season NEC trip in mind in the next twelve months. Amtrak points do not expire for 24 months of activity, but transferring blindly for a trip that may never happen is still a one-way move; a specific route and date test beats "I'll figure it out later."

Chase Ultimate Rewards → Marriott Bonvoy — 70%[4]

Window: September 15 – October 15, 2026 · Base ratio: 1:1 → promo ratio 1:1.7

Chase's record-high transfer bonus to Marriott — the highest ever on this pair per multiple trade desks — continues through Oct 15. 1,000 Chase UR becomes 1,700 Marriott points. The caveat is Marriott's May 2026 chart devaluation and ongoing per-property creep, which has cut effective point value by roughly 10–15% at aspirational properties.[5]

Your move: Transfer only against a specific award you have already priced on Marriott's own search — ideally a property where dynamic pricing is still soft (shoulder-season Ritz-Carltons, mid-category W properties). Do not transfer "to top up for later"; Marriott flash-devalues properties on a near-monthly cadence and the Chase-side ratio is unlikely to drop below 1:1 in the next twelve months.

Amex Membership Rewards → Hilton Honors (US) — 30%[6]

Window: through October 14, 2026 · Base ratio: 1:2 → promo ratio 1:2.6 · May be targeted

Amex's standard 1:2 push to Hilton is bumped 30%, so 1,000 MR becomes 2,600 Hilton Honors. Check your Amex offers page — this one appears to be targeted and does not land in every account.

Your move: If (and only if) today's Bilt 200% does not apply to you and you have a Hilton redemption lined up, 1:2.6 clears roughly 1.3c per MR. Below that bar, Amex MR has better homes — Avios short-haul and Flying Blue remain more valuable per point. Skip if you are not holding an active Hilton booking plan.

Citi ThankYou → Japan Airlines Mileage Bank — 30%[7]

Window: September 20 – October 24, 2026 · Base ratio: 1:1 (premium cards) → promo ratio 1:1.3

JAL became a Citi partner on September 20 with a launch bonus. On premium cards (Strata Elite, Strata Premier, Prestige, AT&T Access More) the ratio is 1:1 base, 1:1.3 with the bonus; no-annual-fee cards transfer at 1:0.7 base, 1:0.9 with the bonus. JAL Mileage Bank is one of the cheapest Oneworld business-class awards into Asia if you can source award space.[8]

Your move: Only transfer if you hold a premium Citi card AND have confirmed JAL or Oneworld partner award space (US→Asia business from ~70k miles). The no-fee-card ratio of 1:0.9 is a wealth-destroyer — skip entirely if that is your card.

Citi ThankYou → Avianca LifeMiles — 25%[9]

Window: September 20 – October 24, 2026 · Base ratio: 1:1 (premium cards) → promo ratio 1:1.25

Citi's second October bonus runs same-window as the JAL offer. LifeMiles remains one of the strongest single Star Alliance programs for US-centric redemptions — no fuel surcharges on Star partners, distance-based pricing that preserves 63,000-mile US→Europe business.[10]

Your move: Transfer against a specific Star Alliance award you have already found space on — do not speculate on LifeMiles balances. The program devalues without notice and has a long history of mid-flight sweet-spot removals; holding miles is strictly worse than holding ThankYou points until you need them.

Program Watch

Marriott Bonvoy — stealth-devaluation cadence continues[5]

Marriott's mid-2026 award-chart revision pushed average pricing up 5–10%, with aspirational flagships (Ritz-Carlton St. Thomas, W Barcelona, JW Marriott Maldives, Edition Reykjavik) up as much as 16%. Per-point value has slipped from roughly 0.8c to roughly 0.7c year-over-year, and property-level flash devaluations continue on a near-monthly basis.[11]

Your move: If you are using today's Chase 70% to Marriott, book inside this window rather than banking points. Marriott-side value is drifting down faster than Chase-side bonuses are drifting up.

Amex Business Platinum — Lufthansa Business Lounge access ends today[12]

Effective October 1, 2026, Amex removes Lufthansa Business Lounge access from the Business Platinum lounge benefit. Priority Pass and Centurion access are unchanged; the removal affects members routing through FRA/MUC on paid tickets without a Star Alliance Gold tier.

Your move: If your travel pattern leaned on this benefit, re-price the card against the new fee stack — the $895 annual fee without the Lufthansa Business perk is a thinner value calculation. No action if you were not using it.

World of Hyatt — five-level pricing bands now live[13]

Hyatt's May 2026 chart restructure expanded each of the eight categories into five levels (Lowest, Low, Moderate, Upper, Top). The published-chart commitment is preserved, but 136 properties shifted categories at launch (82% upward), including 14 that lost Category 1–4 free-night eligibility. Property-level creep has continued through the summer.

Your move: Price every intended Hyatt redemption on the chart before transferring — the five-level structure means a property's advertised category no longer tells you what a specific night will cost. Chase→Hyatt 1:1 remains the best route for Sapphire Reserve holders; Preferred holders now transfer at 4:3 effective October 1 (see yesterday's edition).

Chase Sapphire Preferred → Hyatt — 4:3 cutover landed today[14]

As of today, Chase Sapphire Preferred and Ink Business Preferred cardholders transfer Ultimate Rewards to World of Hyatt at 4:3 rather than 1:1. Sapphire Reserve and Reserve for Business retain 1:1. This change was announced June 15 and has now taken effect.

Your move: If you downgraded from Reserve to Preferred and relied on Hyatt transfers, re-evaluate the card against Bilt (which still transfers 1:1 to Hyatt) or against upgrading back to Reserve. Capital One, Amex, and Citi do not transfer to Hyatt at all.

Today's Play

The Bilt Rent Day asymmetry

Today is the only day of October that Bilt's 200% Hilton / 125% Amtrak transfer bonus is available, and it collides with a long tail of weaker standing offers (Chase 70% Marriott, Amex 30% Hilton, Citi 25–30% to Avianca/JAL) that each run for another 14–23 days. The time-boxed offer is not automatically the better transfer — it is only better if the redemption is already lined up. The right move today, for most readers, is to price every transfer against a confirmed booking before 23:59 PT, not to transfer speculatively because a window closes tonight. Deadline urgency is Bilt's marketing lever; your lever is specificity.

If nothing specific is held: do not transfer today. The Chase→Marriott 70% is live for another two weeks and the Citi→JAL/LifeMiles pair runs through Oct 24. Scarcity makes bad transfers feel urgent; a bad transfer is still a bad transfer.
